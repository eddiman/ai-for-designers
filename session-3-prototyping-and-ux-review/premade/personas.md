## Persona 1: The power user who wants a proactive assistant

**Hypothesis** <!-- One line: who this person is and what they need. Labelled as a hypothesis rather than a fact. -->
A regular rider who already plans trips in a transit app, does not trust the times it shows, and wants the app to learn their regular journeys and warn them on the phone, before they reach the stop, when their bus is delayed or cancelled.

**Grounded in** <!-- Specific evidence from the research: which interview / questionnaire signal. -->
- Wants a personalised app with saved routes and notifications. Maja wants saved favourite routes, automatic recognition of her regular journeys and proactive notifications. In the Questionnaire, 14% chose a personalised app with saved routes and notifications as their single improvement, 34% would use a dedicated app with favourites and notifications, and "a better bus app" got 18 open-text mentions. (Maja, Questionnaire)
- Uses the phone as the main channel. Maja checks a transit app and sometimes a live bus map, and 39% of respondents use a transit app on their phone. (Maja, Questionnaire)
- Wants a phone message when the bus is delayed or cancelled. Maja wants "Your regular bus is delayed." and notifications about cancellations before she reaches the stop. Sofie also wants a message on her phone when a bus is cancelled or disrupted. (Maja, Sofie)
- Uncertainty is the main frustration, and trust is low. Maja is left guessing whether to keep waiting or find another option, and she does not fully trust the information. In the Questionnaire, "not knowing if the bus is actually coming" is the top frustration (31%), and 55% distrust the real-time information they get. (Maja, Questionnaire)
- Builds in extra time. Maja often leaves earlier than necessary, and Sofie often takes an earlier bus than she needs. (Maja, Sofie)
- From one source only: suggestions for alternative routes during a disruption ("Take this alternative route."); "Leave now for your usual bus.", with control over when and how often notifications come; calendar integration, awareness of the day of the week, and recommendations that adjust for meetings, traffic and occupancy; transfers between buses; the mental effort of repeated checking and comparing sources; and the sense that current apps are designed for occasional users. (Maja)
- Overcrowding alerts. The Questionnaire lists crowding only as a secondary concern, with no figure. (Maja, Questionnaire)

**What we still don't know**
- Who the minority in the Questionnaire is. Maja is the only interview that describes this persona in detail, and the Questionnaire reports no results by riding frequency, so "regular commuter" is an assumption taken from Maja.
- Which part of the app the 34% would use. The Questionnaire asks about favourites and notifications in one question, so it cannot say whether they want the personalisation, the alerts, or both.
- Whether personalisation is the need in itself or a way to get early disruption warnings. If the warnings were solved, we do not know how much of the rest of the wish list this persona would still want.
- Whether alternative-route suggestions help. Only Maja raises them, and they stay a hypothesis to test.
- What gap in current apps this persona is reacting to. Maja says apps feel designed for occasional users, Geir says they feel designed for heavy users, and the summaries do not say whether they mean the same app.

## Persona 2: The simplicity-first rider who wants reliable basics

**Hypothesis**
A rider who knows their route, has stopped believing the times they are shown, and wants a plain on time, delayed, cancelled or diverted status where they already look, at the stop or in the app, with no account, setup or extra features.

**Grounded in**
- Does not want more features or setup. Geir does not want accounts, favourites, setup or notifications, and found app setup burdensome. Sofie is not interested in personalisation, saved routes, profiles or large numbers of notifications. In the Questionnaire, 79% agree with "I just want to reliably know when my bus will arrive, without needing extra features.", and 38% would not use a dedicated app with favourites and notifications. (Geir, Sofie, Questionnaire)
- Inaccurate information is worse than the delays. Geir: "It's not the waiting, the not knowing whether to trust the thing in front of you." Sofie says inaccurate information is worse than delays because it stops her making informed decisions. 31% name not knowing if the bus is coming as their top frustration, and 17% name incorrect real-time information. (Geir, Sofie, Questionnaire)
- Has lost trust after buses that never came. Stop displays have shown Geir buses that never arrive. Sofie lost trust after a bus shown in the app never arrived before an important exam. Only 23% of respondents trust the real-time information they get, and one comment reads "I've stopped believing" the app's estimates. (Geir, Sofie, Questionnaire)
- Cannot tell whether a bus is delayed, cancelled or disrupted. Geir waited more than 20 minutes for a bus that never came because no disruption information was given. Sofie cannot tell whether a bus is delayed or cancelled. The Questionnaire has 22 open-text mentions of less waiting without information, 16 of clearer communication about disruptions and cancellations, and the comment "If it's cancelled, tell me." (Geir, Sofie, Questionnaire)
- Wants accurate arrival information above everything else. Geir wants countdowns that reflect when the bus will arrive, and Sofie wants departure information that reflects what is happening. In the Questionnaire, 47% chose accurate real-time arrival information as their single improvement, and one comment reads "Just tell me the truth about when it's coming." (Geir, Sofie, Questionnaire)
- Already knows the route. Geir takes the same Bus 9 from the same stop, and Sofie relies on her own knowledge for familiar journeys. The questionnaire summary concludes that passengers generally know which bus they need. (Geir, Sofie, Questionnaire)
- Where the sources split: Geir checks the timetable and display at the stop (27% use printed timetables and 18% use stop displays), while Sofie checks a transit app at the last minute and wants a phone alert for cancellations. Sofie sits mostly on this side but shares that one wish with the power user. (Geir, Sofie, Questionnaire)
- From one source only: wants uncertainty explained openly instead of times that look certain and turn out wrong, "Make the information you already give people actually true. Forget the bells and whistles." (Geir); normally drives and takes the bus to avoid parking in town (Geir); rides in bad weather, when running late and after evening shifts (Sofie).

**What we still don't know**
- Which channel this persona relies on most. Geir looks at the stop and Sofie looks at her phone. In the Questionnaire, 45% use timetables or displays at the stop and 39% use an app, but the summary does not report which of these groups agreed with the 79% statement.
- Whether this persona wants any notifications. Geir actively dislikes them, while Sofie wants one when a bus is cancelled. The Questionnaire cannot separate these positions because it asks about notifications together with favourites.
- How a phone alert would know which bus to warn about without saved routes or a profile, and what minimum setup Sofie would accept.
- How a stop-based rider would hear about a disruption before leaving for the stop, given that Geir does not want notifications.
- What riders do once they know the bus is not coming. Neither summary describes their alternatives.
- Whether simplicity-first means riding less often. Geir rides about three times a week but calls himself an occasional rider, and the Questionnaire gives no results by riding frequency.
- How many riders sit on both sides. 79% agree with the "without needing extra features" statement and 34% would use a dedicated app with favourites and notifications, so at least 13% of respondents agree with both.
