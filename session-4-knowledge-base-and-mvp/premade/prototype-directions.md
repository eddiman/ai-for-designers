# Prototype directions

> Your work. The 3 prototype directions you generated in Figma Make from your brief packet. Capture what each one is and link to it in Figma.

**Committed problem:** How might we make real-time bus information trustworthy enough that riders feel confident acting on it?

All three directions attack that same trust problem from a different angle. Each is a four-screen mobile flow. [Full prototype set in Figma](https://www.figma.com/design/sbCntnu1vvMCYwhdAZu5tP/AI-Testing?node-id=2227-54080).

---

## Direction 1: Next Nudge
Figma link: [Insert Figma Make prototype link]
What it is: A proactive journey assistant for regular riders. It recognizes a rider’s usual trip and sends a focused alert only when something important changes. The flow shows the delay’s effect on the rider’s plans, recommends when to leave, and optionally presents another travel choice.
How it answers the problem: It communicates disruptions before the rider reaches the stop and translates a delay into a practical decision, such as “Leave at 08:12.” It responds to Maja’s desire for proactive support while keeping personalisation and notifications optional.


## Direction 2: Truth First
Figma link: [Insert Figma Make prototype link]

What it is: A no-account, no-setup status checker for riders who already know their route. It clearly labels each bus as on time, delayed, uncertain, diverted, or cancelled and explains what evidence supports the status.
How it answers the problem: It replaces misleading countdowns with direct answers such as “Yes, it’s coming” or “We can’t confirm this bus.” By showing when vehicle information was last updated and distinguishing live estimates from scheduled times, it helps riders judge whether to wait or leave.


## Direction 3: Stop Signal
Figma link: [Insert Figma Make prototype link]
What it is: A mobile continuation of the physical bus-stop display. Riders scan a code or enter the stop number to see the same live status information on their phone, without creating an account or saving a route.
How it answers the problem: It gives stop-first riders consistent disruption information across the physical display and their phone. Plain-language guidance—such as “Delayed, but still moving” or “Uncertain: live signal lost”—helps riders understand what is happening and make an informed decision.
