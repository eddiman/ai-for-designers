# Persona hypotheses

These personas are hypotheses grounded in the research synthesis. They are not validated user segments.

## The Verification-Seeking Planner

**Need:** Trustworthy, decision-ready information that reduces manual cross-checking.

**Evidence:** This hypothesis is close to Maja, who checks multiple sources, maps, memory, and timing patterns because she does not trust one source enough. The questionnaire reports that 55% of respondents do not trust real-time information.

**Design implications in the artifact:** Explain confidence and uncertainty, show when information was last updated, make cross-source consistency visible, and offer a suggested action.

**Open questions:** Whether the verification is habitual or driven by recent experiences; whether transparent uncertainty would make an app trustworthy; how often this pattern occurs beyond higher-engagement users.

## The Simplicity-First Waiter

**Need:** A simple, visible, trustworthy answer to whether the bus is coming and when, without unnecessary complexity.

**Evidence:** This hypothesis is close to Geir, who relies on physical displays and prefers a minimal interaction. Sofie also prefers simple tools for familiar trips. In the questionnaire, real-time accuracy was a higher priority (47%) than apps or features (14%), and attitudes to new apps were mixed or negative (34% positive; 38% negative).

**Design implications in the artifact:** Use clear stop displays or minimal app screens, plain-language status, no required personalization, and consistency between physical and digital information.

**Open questions:** Why this user may avoid apps; how important physical displays are across user groups; and whether distrust is directed at displays, apps, or the operator.

## The Context-Dependent Rider

**Need:** Minimal information for familiar trips and stronger guidance when uncertainty or unfamiliarity rises.

**Evidence:** This hypothesis is close to Sofie, who needs little support on familiar trips but more reassurance and wayfinding on unfamiliar ones. She adds buffer time because she cannot rely on the system.

**Design implications in the artifact:** Keep a quiet default mode, add guidance for disruption or uncertainty, make alternatives relevant to the situation, and state whether the bus is actively tracked.

**Open questions:** What makes a trip familiar; which contexts raise the need for support; whether context should be inferred or selected; and how common this pattern is.

## Shared tension

The research agrees that trust and reliability come first. It differs on how much help should be layered on top: Maja seeks proactive support, while Geir and Sofie prefer simplicity in many situations.
