## Generic summary
Summary of the Bus Travel Questionnaire

Study overview

142 bus riders completed the survey.
Most respondents were regular bus users, with 53% taking the bus daily or a few times a week.
The survey focused on how people check bus arrivals, what frustrates them, and what would improve their experience.
How people catch the bus today

Most riders rely on some form of arrival information before or while waiting:

39% use a transit app on their phone.
27% use printed timetables at the stop.
18% use real-time displays at the stop.
12% simply show up and wait.

This suggests that passengers already use multiple information sources, but many still depend on physical infrastructure rather than digital tools alone.

What frustrates riders most

The overwhelming theme is uncertainty and lack of trust in bus arrival information.

Top frustrations:

Not knowing if the bus is actually coming (31%)
Buses arriving earlier or later than scheduled (26%)
Incorrect real-time information (17%)

Together, these issues account for 74% of all reported frustrations.

Other concerns such as crowding, confusing routes, or ticketing exist, but are secondary compared to reliability and predictability.

What riders want improved

When asked to choose a single improvement:

47% chose accurate real-time arrival information
21% chose more frequent or reliable service
14% wanted a personalized app with saved routes and notifications

The dominant need is not new functionality, but trustworthy information about when the bus will arrive.

Attitudes toward apps vs. reliable information

The survey shows a clear distinction between wanting better information and wanting more features.

Strong demand for reliable arrival information
79% agree or strongly agree with:

"I just want to reliably know when my bus will arrive, without needing extra features."

This indicates that passengers primarily want certainty and confidence.

Mixed interest in a dedicated bus app
Only 34% agree or strongly agree they would use a dedicated app with favourites and notifications.
38% disagree or strongly disagree.
28% are neutral.

This suggests that advanced app functionality appeals to some users, but is not a universal need.

Low trust in current real-time information
Only 23% trust the real-time arrival information they currently receive.
55% actively distrust it.
22% are neutral.

This is one of the strongest findings in the survey and helps explain why arrival uncertainty is the dominant frustration.

What riders wish existed

The open-text responses reinforce the quantitative findings.

Most common themes:

Confidence that the bus is actually coming (39 mentions)
More accurate real-time predictions (28 mentions)
Less waiting without information (22 mentions)
A better bus app (18 mentions)
Clear communication about disruptions and cancellations (16 mentions)

Representative comments include:

"Just tell me the truth about when it's coming."
"I've stopped believing" the app's estimates.
"If it's cancelled, tell me."
"Standing in the rain not knowing if I've already missed it is the worst part."

These responses show that passengers are not primarily asking for sophisticated planning tools. They want certainty, transparency, and trustworthy communication.

Key insights for design
1. The core problem is uncertainty, not trip planning

Passengers generally know which bus they need. Their challenge is determining whether the bus is coming and when it will arrive.

2. Trust is more important than features

Current real-time systems appear to have a credibility problem. Riders repeatedly express that they would rather have simple but accurate information than feature-rich tools.

3. Reliability drives satisfaction

The strongest improvement opportunity is giving riders confidence in arrival predictions and communicating delays or cancellations clearly.

4. Personalized apps are a secondary need

A segment of users wants saved routes, favourites, and notifications, but this is far less important than improving the accuracy and trustworthiness of arrival information.

Bottom line

The survey paints a remarkably consistent picture: bus riders' biggest need is confidence that the bus is actually coming and accurate information about when it will arrive. Nearly every major finding, from frustrations to desired improvements to open-text comments, points to the same conclusion: reliable real-time information is a far more pressing need than additional app features.

## Directed summary
Summary: What Frustrates Bus Riders and What They Most Want Improved

The questionnaire reveals a very consistent story: the biggest problem with catching the bus today is uncertainty. Riders do not trust the information they receive and often feel unsure whether the bus is actually coming, when it will arrive, or whether they have already missed it.

What frustrates people most

The top frustrations are all related to reliability and information:

31% said their biggest frustration is not knowing whether the bus is actually coming.
26% were most frustrated by buses arriving earlier or later than scheduled.
17% said real-time information is wrong.

Together, these account for 74% of all reported frustrations, making uncertainty by far the dominant issue.

The open-text responses reinforce this pattern:

Many riders said they want confidence that the bus will show up.
Real-time predictions are often seen as unreliable.
Waiting without information, especially in bad weather, is a major pain point.
Riders also expressed frustration when disruptions and cancellations are not communicated clearly.

Typical comments included:

"Just tell me the truth about when it's coming."

"The app says 3 minutes for ten minutes straight."

"If it's cancelled, tell me."

What people most want improved

When asked to choose one improvement:

47% selected accurate real-time arrival information.
21% chose more frequent or more reliable service.
14% chose a personalized app with notifications and saved routes.

The survey suggests that riders are not primarily looking for new features. Instead, they want better information they can trust.

This is reinforced by the agreement-scale questions:

79% agree that they simply want to reliably know when their bus will arrive, without needing extra features.
Only 34% agree they would use a dedicated bus app with favourites and notifications.
55% actively distrust the real-time arrival information they currently receive.
What riders wish existed

The strongest unmet needs are:

Reliable confirmation that the bus is actually coming.
Accurate and trustworthy real-time arrival predictions.
Clear communication about delays, cancellations, and disruptions.
Less waiting without information.
Better apps and notifications, but only if the information is accurate.
Key takeaway

The questionnaire shows that the core problem is trust and certainty, not trip planning. Riders generally know which bus they need. What they struggle with is knowing whether it is coming, when it will arrive, and whether the information they see can be believed. The most valuable improvement would be accurate, trustworthy real-time information that gives passengers confidence when catching the bus.
