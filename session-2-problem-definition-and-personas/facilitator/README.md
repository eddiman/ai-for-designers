# Session 2: facilitator notes

Front-facing material for participants is in the session root `README.md`. This folder holds the facilitator-only guidance.

## Recap (open the session with this)

Whether participants continue from their own Session 1 output or pick up the premade reference versions, open with a recap that walks through the reference summaries:

- **which summary the reference carried forward for each source**, as recorded under **Inputs I chose** in `../premade/triangulation.md`: the directed summaries for Maja and Sofie, and the generic summaries for Geir and the questionnaire. The pack records the choice without a reason, so ask the room which summary they would have carried forward for Maja, and why. Then use Maja's differences analysis in `../premade/interview-summaries.md` to show what the directed prompt did to her: it put words in quotation marks that she never said ("Take this alternative route."), turned "Maybe it knows my calendar" into a definite feature, and dropped her complaint that the 4 is too full at 5pm to get on;
- **what the central tension is**, meaning reliability against personalization (see `../../session-1-research-synthesis/facilitator/research-design-notes.md`). The reference triangulation puts numbers on both sides: Maja is joined by a minority of the questionnaire (14% chose a personalised app, 34% would use one), while Geir, Sofie, and most of the questionnaire want reliable times (47% chose accuracy, 79% want reliable times without extra features);
- **how far the summaries can be trusted.** They separate what was said from what the model concluded, and every figure in the triangulation matches the questionnaire summary. The weak spot is Maja's directed summary, which paraphrases her inside quotation marks, and the triangulation inherits one of those paraphrases ("Leave now for your usual bus"). That makes a useful example for the step 1 check: when an insight rests on a quote, check the quote against `../../session-1-research-synthesis/research/interview-1-maja.md`.

For returning participants the recap is a model for justifying their own work, and for drop-ins it's the context they need to use the premade versions.

## Running the three activities

The participant README gives starter prompts for all three activities (insights → problem definition → personas) and lets participants choose between one chat for the whole packet or a fresh chat per activity, with the evidence pasted in up front either way. Point out the trade-off, carry-over context against clean context, rather than prescribing one. The prompts here are meant to be adapted, unlike Session 1, so steer the room toward tuning them rather than copying them verbatim. The learning is in judging the output, meaning the evidence anchor, the hypothesis label, and the red-team response, rather than in the prompt mechanics.

Rough timeboxes for a live run:

- Recap + setup: 10 min
- Key insights: 10 min
- Problem definition (generate, rank, commit, red-team + response): 25 min
- Personas: 20 min
- Save the packet + wrap: 10 min

## Why personas are required here

Session 3 runs the UX review in character as these personas, so they're a working input the next session depends on. That is the reason they must be evidence-tied and hypothesis-labelled, and it is worth making explicit to the room.
